SUBJECT–VERB AGREEMENT (CONCORD)

Subject–Verb Agreement (also called Concord) refers to the relationship between the subject and the verb in a sentence. The subject and the verb must agree in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.


RULE 1: A SINGULAR SUBJECT TAKES A SINGULAR VERB AND A PLURAL SUBJECT TAKES A PLURAL VERB

Examples

  1. The boy plays football.
  2. Mariama sings well.
  3. The teacher teaches English.
  4. The dog barks loudly.
  5. John goes to school daily.
  6. The girls play football.
  7. The teachers teach English.
  8. The dogs bark loudly.
  9. The boys go to school.
  10. The students read their books.

RULE 2: A SUBJECT COMES BEFORE A PHRASE BEGINNING WITH "OF"

Examples

  1. A bouquet of flowers is on the table.
  2. A herd of cattle is moving.
  3. A basket of oranges was stolen.
  4. A group of students is waiting.
  5. A pile of books is on the desk.
  6. A box of pencils is missing.
  7. A collection of stamps belongs to Musa.
  8. A packet of biscuits has fallen.
  9. A set of tools is expensive.
  10. A bag of rice was bought yesterday.

RULE 3: TWO SINGULAR SUBJECTS CONNECTED BY OR, EITHER...OR, NEITHER...NOR TAKE A SINGULAR VERB

Examples

  1. John or Musa is coming.
  2. Either Mary or Fatmata is responsible.
  3. Neither Musa nor Ibrahim is absent.
  4. Either the teacher or the principal is speaking.
  5. Neither the cat nor the dog is hungry.
  6. John or Mariama has the key.
  7. Either Sia or Kumba is the leader.
  8. Neither Alie nor Tamba was present.
  9. Either the nurse or the doctor is available.
  10. Neither Hawa nor Isatu knows the answer.

RULE 4: THE VERB AGREES WITH THE SUBJECT NEAREST TO IT IN OR / EITHER...OR / NEITHER...NOR SENTENCES

Examples

  1. Neither the teacher nor the students are ready.
  2. Neither the students nor the teacher is ready.
  3. Either the boys or the girl is singing.
  4. Either the girl or the boys are singing.
  5. Neither the dogs nor the cat is sleeping.
  6. Neither the cat nor the dogs are sleeping.
  7. Either the players or the coach is arriving.
  8. Either the coach or the players are arriving.
  9. Neither the books nor the pen is missing.
  10. Neither the pen nor the books are missing.

RULE 5: TWO OR MORE SUBJECTS JOINED BY "AND" TAKE A PLURAL VERB

Examples

  1. John and Mary are classmates.
  2. A pen and a book are on the table.
  3. Musa and Ibrahim play football.
  4. The teacher and the principal are present.
  5. Rice and beans are available.
  6. Fatmata and Hawa sing well.
  7. The boys and girls are dancing.
  8. The cat and dog are sleeping.
  9. Tom and Jerry are friends.
  10. Books and pencils are learning materials.

RULE 6: WHEN TWO SUBJECTS REFER TO ONE PERSON OR THING, USE A SINGULAR VERB

Examples

  1. Rice and stew is my favourite meal.
  2. Fish and chips is delicious.
  3. The owner and manager is absent.
  4. The principal and proprietor is speaking.
  5. Bread and butter is served daily.
  6. Law and order is necessary.
  7. The singer and composer is talented.
  8. The author and editor is present.
  9. Time and tide waits for no one.
  10. The creator and designer is here.

RULE 7: EACH, EVERY, AND NO TAKE A SINGULAR VERB

Examples

  1. Each student has a book.
  2. Each boy plays football.
  3. Every pupil is present.
  4. Every man and woman is invited.
  5. No child is absent.
  6. Each teacher attends the meeting.
  7. Every house has a number.
  8. No person was injured.
  9. Each player receives a prize.
  10. Every candidate writes the examination.

RULE 8: PHRASES LIKE "ALONG WITH", "AS WELL AS", "BESIDES" DO NOT CHANGE THE SUBJECT

Examples

  1. The principal, along with the teachers, is attending.
  2. Musa, as well as his friends, is present.
  3. The girl, besides her sisters, is singing.
  4. The teacher, along with the students, is travelling.
  5. John, as well as Mary, is ready.
  6. The players, together with the coach, are arriving.
  7. The books, along with the pen, are missing.
  8. The workers, besides the manager, are protesting.
  9. The boys, together with their leader, are marching.
  10. The girls, as well as their teacher, are celebrating.

RULE 9: WORDS IN BRACKETS ARE NOT PART OF THE SUBJECT

Examples

  1. Jacob (and his dog) was available.
  2. Musa (with his brothers) is here.
  3. The teacher (and the principal) was invited.
  4. The girl (with her friends) is dancing.
  5. The player (and his coach) was interviewed.
  6. The doctor (with the nurses) is working.
  7. The book (together with the pen) is missing.
  8. The child (and his sister) was rescued.
  9. The principal (along with teachers) is expected.
  10. The man (with his family) is travelling.

RULE 10: IN SENTENCES BEGINNING WITH "HERE" OR "THERE", THE TRUE SUBJECT FOLLOWS THE VERB

Examples

  1. There is a book on the table.
  2. There are many students outside.
  3. There is a problem.
  4. There are five chairs.
  5. Here is the key.
  6. Here are your books.
  7. There is a teacher in the class.
  8. There are two dogs outside.
  9. Here is the answer.
  10. There are many reasons to study.

RULE 11: UNITS OF MEASUREMENT, DISTANCE, MONEY, OR TIME USUALLY TAKE A SINGULAR VERB

When a measurement, distance, amount of money, or period of time is considered as one unit, it takes a singular verb.

Examples

  1. Three miles is a long distance to walk.
  2. Ten years is a long time.
  3. Five thousand leones is enough for the trip.
  4. Two hours is not enough to finish the work.
  5. Twenty litres of water is needed.
  6. Four bags of cement was used for the building.
  7. Thirty minutes is enough for revision.
  8. Seven kilometres is too far to walk.
  9. One hundred thousand leones is a large amount of money.
  10. Five days is enough for the journey.

RULE 12: WITH WORDS THAT INDICATE PORTIONS (ALL, SOME, MOST, A LOT OF, MAJORITY OF), THE VERB DEPENDS ON THE NOUN AFTER "OF"

If the noun after "of" is singular, use a singular verb. If the noun after "of" is plural, use a plural verb.

Examples

  1. All of the water is gone.
  2. Some of the milk has spilled.
  3. A lot of the rice is cooked.
  4. Most of the cake has been eaten.
  5. The majority of the land is fertile.
  6. All of the students are present.
  7. Some of the books are missing.
  8. A lot of the players are injured.
  9. Most of the teachers are attending the workshop.
  10. The majority of the voters are satisfied.

RULE 13: USE "WERE" IN SENTENCES THAT EXPRESS A WISH OR AN UNREAL SITUATION

In sentences that are contrary to fact or express a wish, use were instead of was.

Examples

  1. If I were a doctor, I would help many people.
  2. If she were here, she would be happy.
  3. If he were my brother, I would support him.
  4. If Musa were taller, he could play basketball.
  5. If Mariama were rich, she would travel abroad.
  6. If I were you, I would study harder.
  7. If the weather were better, we would play outside.
  8. If the teacher were present, the lesson would begin.
  9. If Alie were older, he could vote.
  10. If the road were good, the journey would be easier.

RULE 14: GERUNDS USED AS SUBJECTS TAKE A SINGULAR VERB

A gerund is a verb ending in -ing that functions as a noun. When a gerund is the subject of a sentence, it takes a singular verb. When two gerunds are joined by and, they take a plural verb.

Examples

  1. Swimming is good exercise.
  2. Reading helps students learn.
  3. Writing requires practice.
  4. Cooking is an important skill.
  5. Jogging keeps the body fit.
  6. Dancing is enjoyable.
  7. Singing improves confidence.
  8. Playing football is fun.
  9. Swimming and running are healthy activities.
  10. Reading and writing are important language skills.
