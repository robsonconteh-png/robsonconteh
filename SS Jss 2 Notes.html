Population

Meaning of Population

Population refers to the total number of people living in a specific area at a particular time.

Types of Population

  • Underpopulation

    This occurs when the number of people is less than the available resources.


  • Optimum population

    This is a population where the number of people matches the available resources.


  • Overpopulation

    This is where the number of people exceeds the available resources.

Factors Causing Overpopulation

  • High birth rate

    When more babies are born than expected, the population increases quickly over time.


  • Immigration

    When people move into a country from other countries, the population of that country increases.


  • Improved healthcare

    Better hospitals, medicines, and medical services reduce deaths, so more people live longer.


  • Early marriage

    When people marry at a young age, they tend to have more children over their lifetime.


  • Low death rate

    Fewer deaths mean more people survive, which increases the total population.


  • Economic activities

    Areas with jobs and business opportunities attract people, increasing population in those places.


  • Illiteracy

    Lack of education can lead to poor family planning, resulting in larger families and faster population growth

Population Distribution in Sierra Leone

Some areas are densely populated while others are sparsely populated.

Densely Populated Areas

  • Kenema
  • Kono
  • Bo
  • Makeni
  • Freetown

Reasons for Densely Populated Areas

  • Mining

    People move to mining areas because of job opportunities and income from minerals like diamonds and gold.

  • Farming

    Fertile land attracts people for agriculture and food production.

  • good roads

    Easy transport encourages trade and movement, so more people settle there.
  • health facilities

    Availability of hospitals and clinics improves living conditions and attracts people.

  • industries

    Factories provide jobs, so workers migrate to those areas.

  • trade

    Markets and business activities attract traders and customers.

  • schools

    Educational institutions bring students, teachers, and families.

  • jobs

    General employment opportunities make people move from rural to urban areas.

Problems of Densely Populated Areas

  • Health Issues

    Overcrowding leads to diseases spreading easily and poor sanitation.


  • poor living standards

    Many people live in slums with limited access to clean water and basic services.


  • crime

    High population and unemployment can lead to theft, robbery, and other crimes.


  • high housing cost

    Demand for houses is high, so rent and land prices increase.


  • prostitution

    Lack of jobs and poverty may push some people into risky survival activities.

Sparsely Populated Areas

  • Koinadugu
  • Bonthe
  • Moyamba
  • Tonkolili
  • Pujehun

Reasons for Sparsely Populated Areas

  • Few job opportunities

    There are limited jobs, so people move to towns and cities for work.


  • Poor roads

    Bad roads make transport difficult and discourage settlement.


  • Lack of industries

    Few factories or businesses mean less employment and development.


  • Poor social services

    Limited access to schools, hospitals, and clean water reduces population.


  • Poor farming conditions

    Unfertile soil, dry land, or flooding makes farming difficult.

Population Census

A census is the official counting of all people in a country.

Importance of Census

  • Planning

    Census helps government plan schools, hospitals, and roads constructions based on population size.


  • Resource distribution

    It helps in fair sharing of national resources among regions.


  • Taxes

    It provides data for planning tax collection and government revenue.


  • Unemployment estimation

    It helps to know how many people are jobless in a country.


  • Policy making

    Assists government in making good decisions and policies.

Problems of Census

  • Political interference

    Some people may give false information for political reasons.


  • High cost

    Conducting a census is expensive.


  • Poor communication

    Some areas are difficult to reach or lack communication systems.


  • Illiteracy

    Some people cannot read or understand census questions properly.

  • Difficult access

    Remote areas make data collection hard.

Types of Census

  • De facto

    Counting people based on where they are physically present during the census.


  • De jure

    Counting people based on their usual place of residence.

Data Collection Methods

  • Census – A census is the official counting of all people in a country at a particular time. It provides information about population size, age, sex, occupation, and location.

  • Civil Registration – This is the continuous recording of important life events such as births, deaths, marriages, and divorces by government authorities.

  • Health Records – These are records collected by hospitals, clinics, and health centers about births, deaths, diseases, and other health-related information.

  • Sample Surveys – A sample survey collects information from a selected group of people who represent the larger population. It is faster and less expensive than a census.

Institutions for Population Data

  • Statistics Sierra Leone – This is the government agency responsible for conducting censuses, surveys, and producing official population statistics.

  • National Electoral Commission – Collects voter registration data which helps provide information about the voting-age population.

  • Immigration Department – Records information about people entering and leaving the country, helping track migration patterns.

  • Births and Deaths Registry – Registers all births and deaths in the country and provides vital population statistics.

  • National Registration Office – Issues national identification cards and maintains records of citizens and residents.
