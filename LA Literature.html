INTRODUCTION TO LITERATURE

In general term the word literature could mean any written work. However, if we are to narrow down our definition to mean the subject of literature that is studied at school, we can therefore say that literature is any writing that possesses literary merit. This type of literature is imaginative. It is the product of the writer’s imagination

BRANCHES OF LITERATURE

They are:
  • prose
  • drama
  • poetry

PROSE

Prose is the ordinary form of writing and speaking that people use every day. Unlike poetry, it is written in sentences and paragraphs and does not follow a special rhythm or pattern.

Characteristics of Prose

  • Written in paragraphs: Prose is arranged in paragraphs, not in lines and stanzas like poetry.
  • Uses ordinary language: It uses normal sentences to express ideas, events, and feelings.
  • Different points of view: A story may be told by the writer using "I" (first person) or by talking about others using "he", "she", or "they" (third person).
  • Written by an author: The person who writes a prose work is called an author or writer.
  • Has a theme: Every prose work has a main idea or message that the writer wants readers to understand.

Types of Prose

1. Fiction

Fiction is a story created from the writer's imagination. The characters and events are not real, although they may seem real.

Examples:Novels and short stories.

Examples of books:

  • Who is to Blame? by Alhaji Mohamed Abu Sesay
  • So Long a Letter by Mariama Bâ

2. Non-Fiction

Non-fiction is based on real people, real events, and true information.

Examples: Autobiographies, biographies, essays, newspapers, and reports.

DRAMA

Drama is a type of literature that is written to be acted on a stage before an audience. When actors perform a drama, it is called a play.

Characteristics of Drama

  • Drama is divided into acts and scenes.
  • It has different characters who speak to one another through dialogue.
  • Drama is meant to be performed on stage.
  • The person who writes a drama is called a dramatist or playwright.
  • Every drama has a theme, which is the main idea or message the writer wants to pass across.

Types of Drama

There are different types of drama, including:

  • Tragedy
  • Comedy
  • Tragic Comedy
  • Melodrama

1. Tragedy

A tragedy is a serious play that ends sadly. The main character often suffers or dies because of a mistake or weakness.

Examples:

  • Macbeth by William Shakespeare
  • The Gods Are Not to Blame by Ola Rotimi

2. Comedy

A comedy is a funny play that makes people laugh and usually ends happily.

Example:

  • The Taming of the Shrew by William Shakespeare

3. Tragic Comedy

A tragic comedy combines both serious and funny events. It has elements of both tragedy and comedy.

Example:

  • The Tempest by William Shakespeare

4. Melodrama

A melodrama is a play that includes songs and music. It creates strong emotions such as joy, sadness, fear, or excitement and usually ends happily.

POETRY

Poetry is a piece of writing in which the words are chosen for their sounds and the images they suggest. Poets use words not just for their obvious meanings. The words are arranged in lines with repeated rhythm. This is called stanzas.

Features/Characteristics of Poetry

  • A poem is written in lines and stanzas.
  • A poem economises on words.
  • A poem uses sound devices and other literary devices.
  • A poem uses rhyme scheme.
  • The person who writes a poem is called a poet.
  • One important element of poetry is the theme. This is the central idea or underlying message of the poet.

Types of Poetry

  • Epic
  • Satire
  • Lyric
  • Sonnet
  • Ode
  • Elegy
  • Idyll
  • Ballad
  • Didactic Poem
  • Limerick

Epic

A long narrative poem that describes the deeds of a great hero and reflects the values of a culture.

Satire

A type of poem that ridicules the foolish actions of a person, society, or institution.

Lyric

A poem full of emotions and strong feelings. It is usually short and meant to be sung.

Sonnet

A lyrical poem of fourteen lines. It is divided into two parts: the octave (first eight lines) and the sestet (last six lines).

Ode

A type of poetry that is in the form of an address, usually directed to a person or thing for a specific purpose.

Elegy

A poem of mourning, usually over the death of an individual or the passing of life and beauty.

Idyll

A short poem that describes a scene or incident, often showing simple rural life.

Ballad

A story told in verse, often meant to be sung.

Didactic Poem

A poem that teaches moral and social lessons.

Limerick

A humorous poem made up of five lines.

LITERARY DEVICES AND FIGURES OF SPEECH

Literary devices are techniques or tools used by writers to make their writing more interesting, effective, and meaningful.

Figures of speech are words or expressions used in a special or imaginative way rather than in their ordinary meaning.

1. Simile

A simile is a direct comparison between two different things using "like" or "as".

Examples:

  1. The dog is as black as charcoal.
  2. Lamin was like a lion in the battle.
  3. She is as busy as a bee.
  4. The baby slept like a log.
  5. His face shone like the sun.

2. Metaphor

A metaphor is a direct comparison between two unlike things without using "like" or "as".

Examples:

  1. Bockarie was a lion in the fight.
  2. The world is a stage.
  3. Time is money.
  4. Her voice is music to my ears.
  5. Life is a journey.

3. Personification

Personification is giving human qualities to animals, objects, or ideas.

Examples:

  1. The flowers nodded.
  2. The thunder grumbled.
  3. The wind whispered through the trees.
  4. The sun smiled at us.
  5. The stars danced in the sky.

4. Irony

Irony occurs when what happens is different from what is expected.

Examples:

  1. The Titanic was said to be unsinkable but sank on its first voyage.
  2. A traffic policeman was fined for breaking traffic rules.
  3. A fire station caught fire.
  4. A doctor became seriously ill.
  5. A lifeguard needed rescuing.

5. Hyperbole

Hyperbole is deliberate exaggeration for emphasis.

Examples:

  1. I've told you a thousand times.
  2. Thanks a million.
  3. I am so hungry I could eat a horse.
  4. The bag weighs a ton.
  5. She cried a river of tears.

6. Euphemism

A euphemism is a mild or polite expression used instead of a harsh or unpleasant one.

Examples:

  1. The old man has kicked the bucket.
  2. Salamatu is economical with the truth.
  3. He passed away.
  4. She is visually impaired.
  5. He is between jobs.

7. Alliteration

Alliteration is the repetition of the same beginning sound in nearby words.

Examples:

  1. She sells sea shells on the sea shore.
  2. Fred fried fish on Friday.
  3. Peter Piper picked a peck of peppers.
  4. Busy bees buzzed by.
  5. Big black bugs bit Ben.

8. Onomatopoeia

Onomatopoeia refers to words that imitate natural sounds.

Examples:

  1. Buzz
  2. Bang
  3. Hiss
  4. Splash
  5. Crack

9. Synecdoche

Synecdoche occurs when a part represents the whole or the whole represents a part.

Examples:

  1. Hired hands. (workers)
  2. Many mouths to feed. (people)
  3. All hands on deck. (sailors)
  4. We need more heads in the meeting. (people)
  5. New wheels. (car)

10. Oxymoron

An oxymoron is a combination of two words with opposite meanings.

Examples:

  1. Cruel kindness.
  2. Painful laughter.
  3. Bittersweet.
  4. Deafening silence.
  5. Clearly confused.

11. Soliloquy

A soliloquy is a speech made by a character who is alone on stage, expressing his or her thoughts aloud.

OTHER LITERARY TERMS

Fiction

A story created from the writer's imagination.

Non-Fiction

Writing based on real events and facts.

Sonnet

A poem of fourteen lines. The first eight lines are called the Octave and the last six lines are called the Sestet.

Elegy

A poem that expresses sadness, usually for someone who has died.

Comedy

A literary work that is humorous and usually ends happily.

Tragedy

A literary work that ends sadly, often with suffering or death.

Biography

The life story of a person written by another person.

Autobiography

The life story of a person written by himself or herself.

Theme

The main idea or message in a literary work.

Diction

The choice of words used by a writer.

Setting

The time and place in which events occur in a literary work.

Character

A person, animal, or being that takes part in a literary work.

Climax

The highest point of action or excitement in a literary work.

Characterisation

The way a writer develops and presents characters in a literary work.

Plot

The sequence or arrangement of events in a story.

Narrative Technique

The method used by a writer to tell a story.

First-Person Narrative

A narrative that uses "I" or "we" to tell the story.

Third-Person Narrative

A narrative that uses "he", "she", "it", or "they" to tell the story.

Epistolary Technique

A method of telling a story through letters.

Example: So Long a Letter by Mariama Bâ.

Fable

A story in which animals speak and behave like human beings.

Anthology

A collection of poems, stories, or literary works.

Stanza

A group of lines forming a unit in a poem.

Playwright

A person who writes a play.

Poet

A person who writes poetry.

Author

A person who writes prose works such as novels and short stories.
