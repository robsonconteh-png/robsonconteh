SYNONYMS AND ANTONYMS

Synonyms and antonyms are important parts of vocabulary development. When we listen to people speak or read books, newspapers, and other materials, we often come across unfamiliar words. Learning synonyms and antonyms helps us understand these words and improves our speaking and writing skills.

The more words learners know, the easier it becomes for them to express their ideas clearly and confidently.

NOTE

Synonyms and antonyms are not just about memorizing examples. The examples are only there to help you understand the meaning. Before choosing a synonym or antonym, consider the following:

  1. Context (Meaning in the Sentence)
    A word may have different meanings depending on how it is used. Always read the whole sentence before choosing an answer.
  2. Part of Speech
    The replacement word must belong to the same part of speech.
    • Noun → Noun
    • Verb → Verb
    • Adjective → Adjective
    • Adverb → Adverb
    Example:
    Teacher (noun) → Educator (noun)
    Run (verb) → Sprint (verb)
  3. Tense or Form of the Word
    The replacement word must fit the tense of the sentence.
    4. Example:
    • Present: walk → stroll
    • Past: walked → strolled
    • Future: will walk → will stroll
  4. Singular and Plural Forms
    If the word is singular, the answer should also be singular. If it is plural, the answer should also be plural.
  5. Positive or Negative Meaning
    Make sure the word keeps the intended meaning of the sentence.
  6. Use of Prefixes and Suffixes
    Many antonyms are formed by adding prefixes such as:
    • un- (happy → unhappy)
    • dis- (agree → disagree)
    • in- (visible → invisible)
    • im- (possible → impossible)
    • ir- (regular → irregular)

Remember: In examinations, do not choose a synonym or antonym simply because you have memorized it. Always look at the context, part of speech, tense, and meaning of the sentence first.
