CONTINUOUS WRITING

Think of continuous writing as simply telling a story or explaining an idea in a natural, flowing way. In continuous writing, you connect your sentences into smooth paragraphs. It is all about linking your thoughts together so that your writing feels like a complete, easy-to-follow conversation.


Guidelines for Effective Continuous Writing

Content

The most important step in writing is to fully understand what the question is asking. Before you begin, make sure you clearly know what is required. If you write about something unrelated to the topic, your work will be considered out of context, which will lead to a loss of marks.

Organization

How you arrange your ideas is very important in producing a good piece of writing. You should follow these guidelines:

  • Use Paragraphs
    Divide your work into clear and well-organized paragraphs.

  • One Idea per Paragraph
    Each paragraph should focus on a single main point.

  • Use Linking Words
    Ensure your ideas flow smoothly by using words such as firstly, secondly, in addition, furthermore, moreover, however, and for example.

  • Follow the Required Format
    If you are writing a letter, report, or any specific format, make sure you include all necessary parts such as addresses, greetings, and appropriate endings.

Expression

This is a key part of your assessment. It focuses on how well you use the English language. To do well in this area:

  • Use a variety of sentence structures, including simple, compound, and complex sentences.

  • Focus on clarity so your meaning is easy for the reader to understand.

  • Pay attention to correct tense usage to avoid confusion.

Mechanical Accuracy

This area deals with the technical correctness of your writing. To improve your accuracy, always check for:

  • Correct spelling of words
  • Proper and consistent use of tenses
  • Correct punctuation to make your writing clear and easy to read

Sentence

A sentence is a group of words that are arranged to make complete sense. A complete sentence must have a subject and a predicate. Every sentence begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop (.), question mark (?), or exclamation mark (!).

Types of Sentence

We have three (3) types of sentence. They are:
  1. The Simple Sentence
  2. The Compound Sentence
  3. The Complex Sentence

The Simple Sentence

A simple sentence contains one subject and one predicate and expresses one complete idea.
Examples:
  1. The students studied very hard.
  2. Fatmata went to school yesterday.
  3. The girls are beautiful.
  4. John is reading a book.
  5. The teacher entered the class.
  6. My father cooks rice.
  7. The dog barked loudly.
  8. She sings very well.
  9. The boys played football.
  10. The sun is shining brightly.

The Compound Sentence

A compound sentence is made up of two simple sentences joined by a coordinating conjunction such as and, but, or, so. Examples:
  1. Fatmata swept the compound and Marie washed the dishes.
  2. Joseph opened the bag and took out a book.
  3. Kadi is good at English but not very good at Math.
  4. It started to rain and we went inside.
  5. I wanted to go out but it was raining.
  6. She studied hard so she passed the exam.
  7. John cooked food and his sister set the table.
  8. We can go to the park or we can stay at home.
  9. The teacher explained the lesson and the students listened carefully.
  10. Musa was tired so he went to bed early.

The Complex Sentence

A complex sentence is formed by joining a main clause with one or more subordinate clauses using subordinating conjunctions such as because, since, although, if, when, while, before, after, unless, until. Examples:
  1. Amie stayed in bed because she had a fever.
  2. Musu arrived late because she missed the bus.
  3. I will go out if it stops raining.
  4. She smiled when she saw her friend.
  5. Although it was cold, they went to school.
  6. He passed the exam because he studied hard.
  7. We stayed inside while it was raining.
  8. After the lesson ended, the students went home.
  9. I will wait here until you return.
  10. Unless you work hard, you will not succeed.

PARAGRAPH WRITING

A paragraph is a group of related sentences that develop one main idea. This main idea is usually introduced in the first sentence, known as the topic sentence. The sentences that follow develop, explain, and support the main idea.

paragraph is generally made up of several sentences and may vary in length depending on the purpose of the writing and the amount of information being presented. Paragraphs play an important role in writing because they help organize ideas in a clear and logical manner, making it easier for the reader to follow and understand the writer’s thoughts.

Paragraph Structure

A paragraph can be divided into three main parts:

  1. Topic Sentence
  2. Supporting Sentences
  3. Concluding Sentence

Topic Sentence (The Beginning)

The topic sentence introduces the main idea of the paragraph. Guidelines:
  • State one main idea clearly.
  • Place the most important information at the beginning.
  • Let the reader know what the paragraph will discuss.

Supporting Sentences (The Middle)

The supporting sentences develop and explain the idea introduced in the topic sentence. Guidelines:
  • Provide details that support the main idea.
  • Give explanations where necessary.
  • Include evidence or relevant information.
  • Show how the supporting details relate to the topic sentence.
  • Maintain focus on the main idea throughout the paragraph.

Concluding Sentence (The End)

The concluding sentence brings the paragraph to a proper close. Guidelines:
  • Reinforce the main idea of the paragraph.
  • Link back to the topic sentence.
  • Provide a sense of completion.
  • Prepare the reader for the next paragraph or idea.
EXAMPLES

Write a letter to the Ministry of Education

Paragraph 1

Infrastructure
Topic Sentence: I am writing to request improved infrastructure for our school.


Supporting Sentences: The school lacks adequate classroom space, and many students study in overcrowded classrooms. In addition, some buildings are old and require renovation, while the shortage of desks and chairs makes learning uncomfortable. Improving these facilities would create a more suitable environment for effective teaching and learning.


Concluding Sentence: I therefore appeal to the Ministry of Education to provide the necessary infrastructure for our school.


Complete Paragraph: I am writing to request improved infrastructure for our school. The school lacks adequate classroom space, and many students study in overcrowded classrooms. In addition, some buildings are old and require renovation, while the shortage of desks and chairs makes learning uncomfortable. Improving these facilities would create a more suitable environment for effective teaching and learning. I therefore appeal to the Ministry of Education to provide the necessary infrastructure for our school.


Paragraph 2:


Teachers

Topic Sentence: Another major challenge facing our school is the shortage of qualified teachers.


Supporting Sentences: Several subjects are taught by a limited number of teachers, making it difficult to give students the attention they need. As a result, some classes are combined, while others receive fewer lessons than required. Employing more qualified teachers would improve the quality of education and enhance students' academic performance.


Concluding Sentence: I therefore kindly request the Ministry of Education to recruit more teachers for our school.


Complete Paragraph: Another major challenge facing our school is the shortage of qualified teachers. Several subjects are taught by a limited number of teachers, making it difficult to give students the attention they need. As a result, some classes are combined, while others receive fewer lessons than required. Employing more qualified teachers would improve the quality of education and enhance students' academic performance. I therefore kindly request the Ministry of Education to recruit more teachers for our school

COMPOSITION

A composition is a piece of writing that expands a topic or idea. In examinations, students may be asked to write different types of compositions such as narrative and descriptive compositions, among others.

Types of Composition
  1. Narrative Composition
  2. Descriptive Composition

Narrative Composition

A narrative composition is a story or an account of past events. When writing a narrative composition, the title should be written clearly at the top of the page and underlined. Ideas should be arranged into meaningful paragraphs. Simple and compound sentences should be used to express ideas clearly.


A narrative composition is divided into three main parts:
  • Introduction
  • Body
  • Conclusion

Introduction

The introduction is usually short and states the purpose of the writing. It should attract the reader’s attention and make them interested in reading further.

Body

The body is the main part of the composition and usually contains 2–3 paragraphs. This is where the writer gives full details of the events. The narration should be lively and clear, explaining what happened before, during, and after the event.

Conclusion

The conclusion is a short ending that shows how the writer felt and the impact of the event.
In narrative writing, the simple past tense is mostly used because the events have already taken place.

Examples

A Visit to the Village

Introduction:

Last December, I visited my village for the first time in many years. It was one of the most interesting experiences I have ever had.

Body:

Before the journey, I packed my clothes and prepared for the long trip. When I arrived, I was warmly welcomed by my relatives. I spent time visiting different places in the village, including the farm and the river. I also took part in cultural activities and enjoyed traditional meals with my family.

Conclusion:

The visit made me very happy, and I promised to return again during the next holiday.

Example 2:

My School Sports Day

Introduction:

Last term, our school organised a sports day that was full of excitement and competition.

Body:

The event started early in the morning with a march past by all the houses. Different races such as the 100 metres, relay, and sack race were held. I took part in the relay race for my class. The crowd cheered loudly as students competed in various events, and points were recorded for each house.

Conclusion:

At the end of the day, my house won second position, and I felt proud of my participation.

Descriptive Composition

A descriptive composition is a type of writing that describes a person, object, or scene in detail. It appeals to the reader’s sense of sight and helps them to imagine what is being described. The title should also be clearly written and underlined at the top of the page.


A descriptive composition focuses on the following:
  • Describing a Person
  • Describing an Object
  • Describing a Scene

Describing a Person

When describing a person, attention should be given to: Size and height, Age (if known), Complexion, Eyes, nose, and mouth General appearance, Character or personality (if known).

Describing an Object

When describing an object, consider: Size, Colour, Shape and height, General appearance, Where it is usually found or located.

Describing a Scene

When describing a scene, consider: The environment, General appearance, People present (population), Activities or occupation of the people, What is happening at the scene.

Example 1:

Describing a Teacher

Topic Sentence:

My English teacher is one of the most respected teachers in our school.

Supporting Details:

He is tall and well-built with a dark complexion. He speaks clearly and always explains lessons in a simple way. He is very patient with students and always encourages us to work hard. He also dresses neatly and behaves respectfully towards everyone.

Concluding Sentence:

Because of his qualities, he is loved by many students in the school.

Example 2:

Describing a Market Scene

Topic Sentence:

The central market in our town is always busy and lively.

Supporting Details:

Many traders display their goods such as vegetables, fruits, clothes, and fish. Customers move from one stall to another bargaining for lower prices. The noise of people calling out and vehicles passing makes the environment very active. The smell of fresh food fills the air.

Concluding Sentence:

The market is an important place where people buy and sell goods every day.

Creative Story Writing

Creative story writing is a type of composition where you are expected to write a story based on a given instruction. The question may ask you to begin or end your story with a particular saying or sentence. In this type of writing, you are expected to be imaginative while making your story sound realistic and convincing.

When writing a creative story, the following guidelines should be followed:
  • Write the title clearly at the top of the page.
  • Divide your ideas into paragraphs.
  • Use simple and compound sentences.
  • Ensure your story is clear, logical, and believable.

Structure of a Creative Story

Introduction

This is the beginning of the story. It introduces the setting, characters, and the situation that leads to the main events

Body

This is the main part of the story. It describes the events in detail, showing what happens step by step in a clear and interesting way.

Conclusion

This is the ending of the story. It shows how the story ends and often includes the lesson learned or the final outcome.

Example 1:

“So it all started……”

Introduction:

So it all started on a rainy Monday morning when I was getting ready for school.

Body:

I left home late because I could not find my books. On my way to school, I met an old man who dropped his bag, and I helped him pick it up. He thanked me and gave me some money. When I got to school, I was late and had to explain myself to the teacher. Surprisingly, the teacher understood and allowed me into class.

Conclusion:

That day taught me that kindness can bring unexpected rewards, and so it all started as a difficult morning but ended as a memorable experience.

Example 2:

“It pays to be honest.”

Introduction:

It pays to be honest, and I learned this lesson through a very important experience in my life.

Body:

One day, I found a wallet on my way home from school. It contained a large amount of money. At first, I thought of keeping it, but I decided to take it to the owner instead. I reported it to the school authority, and they helped locate the owner.

Conclusion:

The owner was very grateful and rewarded me for my honesty. From that day, I understood that it truly pays to be honest.
