DIRECT AND INDIRECT SPEECH

What is Direct Speech?

Direct speech gives the exact words spoken by a person. The speaker’s words are written inside quotation marks (" ").

Examples

  • Mom said, "Where are my keys?"
  • Ibrahim said, "I feel ill."
  • The teacher said, "The principal is attending a meeting."

What is Indirect Speech?

Indirect speech, also called reported speech, tells what someone said without using the speaker's exact words.

Words such as said, told, and asked are commonly used when reporting speech.

Examples

  • Direct: Ibrahim said, "I feel ill."
  • Indirect: Ibrahim said that he felt ill.

Differences Between Direct and Indirect Speech

  1. Quotation marks are not used in indirect speech.
  2. The tense of the verb usually changes.
  3. Pronouns may change.
  4. Some time and place words may also change.

Examples

Direct Speech Indirect Speech
Umaru said, "It is time to leave." Umaru said that it was time to leave.
Alice said, "I cannot find my book." Alice said that she could not find her book.

Reporting Commands and Instructions

When reporting commands or instructions, verbs such as order, tell, and warn are used.

Examples

  • Direct: The teacher said, "Stop running in the corridor!"
  • Indirect: The teacher ordered the pupils to stop running in the corridor.
  • Direct: Mr. Bockarie said, "Put your books away."
  • Indirect: Mr. Bockarie told the children to put their books away.

Reporting Questions

The verb ask is usually used when reporting questions.

Examples

  • Direct: Sally said, "Where is my umbrella?"
  • Indirect: Sally asked where her umbrella was.
  • Direct: Aminata said, "Have you finished your homework?"
  • Indirect: Aminata asked if I had finished my homework.

Question-Word Questions

When reporting questions beginning with words such as what, where, when, and how, keep the same question word.

Examples

  • Direct: Mom said, "Where are your shoes?"
  • Indirect: Mom asked where my shoes were.
  • Direct: Susan asked, "What time does the show start?"
  • Indirect: Susan asked what time the show started.

Yes / No Questions

Use if or whether when reporting questions that can be answered with yes or no.

Examples

  • Direct: Tamba asked, "Is it raining?"
  • Indirect: Tamba asked if it was raining.
  • Direct: Alice asked, "Can you help us?"
  • Indirect: Alice asked whether I could help them.

Reporting Orders, Commands and Requests

Orders and requests are usually reported with tell, ask, order, command, or request followed by to + verb.

Examples

  • Direct: "Call me back later," he told me.
  • Indirect: He told me to call him back later.
  • Direct: "Stand up!" commanded the teacher.
  • Indirect: The teacher commanded the students to stand up.
  • Direct: "Could you call me back please?" requested the principal.
  • Indirect: The principal asked the student to call him back later.

DIRECT AND INDIRECT SPEECH: STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE WITH EXAMPLES

HOW TO CHANGE DIRECT SPEECH TO INDIRECT SPEECH

Follow these steps:

  1. Remove quotation marks.
  2. Use reporting verbs like said, told, asked.
  3. Change the tense one step back (present → past).
  4. Change pronouns (I → he/she, we → they).
  5. Change time words (now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day).

EXAMPLES: DIRECT → INDIRECT (WITH EXPLANATION)

  1. Direct: “I am happy today,” she said.
    Step: am → was, today → that day, I → she
    Indirect: She said that she was happy that day.

  2. Direct: “We are going to school now,” they said.
    Step: are going → were going, now → then, we → they
    Indirect: They said that they were going to school then.

  3. Direct: “I have finished my work,” Musa said.
    Step: have finished → had finished
    Indirect: Musa said that he had finished his work.

  4. Direct: “I will help you,” he said.
    Step: will → would, you → me (depending on speaker)
    Indirect: He said that he would help me.

  5. Direct: “We must obey the rules,” the teacher said.
    Step: must → had to, we → they
    Indirect: The teacher said that they had to obey the rules.

HOW TO CHANGE INDIRECT SPEECH TO DIRECT SPEECH

Follow these steps:

  1. Add quotation marks.
  2. Change reporting verbs (said that → said).
  3. Return tense back to present.
  4. Change pronouns back (he/she → I, they → we).
  5. Change time words (then → now, that day → today).

EXAMPLES: INDIRECT → DIRECT (WITH EXPLANATION)

  1. Indirect: She said that she was tired.
    Step: was → am, she → I
    Direct: “I am tired,” she said.

  2. Indirect: He said that he had lost his pen.
    Step: had lost → have lost, he → I
    Direct: “I have lost my pen,” he said.

  3. Indirect: They said that they were going home.
    Step: were going → are going, they → we
    Direct: “We are going home,” they said.

  4. Indirect: Mary said that she would come the next day.
    Step: would come → will come, the next day → tomorrow
    Direct: “I will come tomorrow,” Mary said.

  5. Indirect: The teacher said that the pupils had finished the work.
    Step: had finished → finished
    Direct: “You finished the work,” the teacher said.

NOTE:

Always ask yourself:

  • What tense is used?
  • What pronoun is used?
  • What time word is used?

If you answer these three questions correctly, you will never make mistakes in reported speech.

QUESTION TAGS

WHAT IS A QUESTION TAG?

A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement. It is used to ask for agreement or confirmation.

Example:

  • You are a student, aren’t you?
  • Mary isn’t a teacher, is she?

STEP-BY-STEP RULES FOR FORMING QUESTION TAGS

  1. Identify if the statement is positive or negative.
  2. If the statement is positive, the question tag must be negative.
  3. If the statement is negative, the question tag must be positive.
  4. Use the correct auxiliary verb (is, are, was, do, does, have, etc.).
  5. Repeat the subject using a pronoun (he, she, they, we, it).

NOTE:

Important Rule:
A sentence that has “not” is a negative sentence.
A sentence that does not have “not” is a positive sentence.

This helps you to easily choose the correct question tag:

  • For Positive sentence choose negative question tag
  • For Negative sentence choose Positive question tag

AUXILIARY VERBS (POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE FORMS)

Positive Form Negative Form
am / is / are am not / isn’t / aren’t
was / were wasn’t / weren’t
do / does / did don’t / doesn’t / didn’t
have / has / had haven’t / hasn’t / hadn’t
will won’t
can can’t
shall shan’t
may may not
must must not

Tip: Always look for the helping verb first before forming a question tag. If there is no helping verb, use do / does / did.


STEP 1: POSITIVE STATEMENTS → NEGATIVE TAGS

Examples

  1. Statement: You are a student.
    Step: positive → negative tag, are → aren’t, you stays you
    Answer: You are a student, aren’t you?

  2. Statement: She is very happy.
    Step: is → isn’t, she stays she
    Answer: She is very happy, isn’t she?

  3. Statement: They were late.
    Step: were → weren’t, they stays they
    Answer: They were late, weren’t they?

  4. Statement: You speak English.
    Step: do → don’t, you stays you
    Answer: You speak English, don’t you?

  5. Statement: He has a book.
    Step: has → hasn’t, he stays he
    Answer: He has a book, hasn’t he?

STEP 2: NEGATIVE STATEMENTS → POSITIVE TAGS

Examples

  1. Statement: You are not a teacher.
    Step: are not → are, you stays you
    Answer: You are not a teacher, are you?

  2. Statement: She isn’t well.
    Step: isn’t → is, she stays she
    Answer: She isn’t well, is she?

  3. Statement: They were not late.
    Step: weren’t → were, they stays they
    Answer: They were not late, were they?

  4. Statement: You do not like sugar.
    Step: do not → do, you stays you
    Answer: You do not like sugar, do you?

  5. Statement: Ansu didn’t come.
    Step: didn’t → did, Ansu → he
    Answer: Ansu didn’t come, did he?

STEP 3: SENTENCES WITHOUT AUXILIARY VERBS

When there is no auxiliary (helping) verb in the sentence, we use do / does / did to form the question tag.

  • Use does / doesn’t for present tense (he/she/it)
  • Use do / don’t for I/we/you/they
  • Use did / didn’t for past tense

Examples

  1. Statement: They came yesterday.
    Answer: They came yesterday, didn’t they?
  2. Statement: She goes to school daily.
    Answer: She goes to school daily, doesn’t she?
  3. Statement: Kadiatu comes late.
    Answer: Kadiatu comes late, doesn’t she?
  4. Statement: They visited their grandmother.
    Answer: They visited their grandmother, didn’t they?
  5. Statement: Musa plays football.
    Answer: Musa plays football, doesn’t he?
