Letter Writing

Letter writing is an important part of composition writing. It is used to communicate ideas, requests, feelings, or information to a specific person or group. In examinations, letters are divided into three main types:

Formal Letter
Informal Letter
Semi-formal Letter

Every letter must have three main parts:

Introduction
Body
Conclusion

Formal Letter
Semi-formal Letter

FORMAL LETTER

A formal letter is a serious type of letter written to someone in authority. The language must be respectful, clear, and direct.

A formal letter is a serious type of letter written to someone in authority. The language must be respectful, clear, and direct.

Introduction (Opening Paragraph)

The introduction must:
  • Begin with a respectful greeting (Dear Sir/Madam)

  • Show courtesy or respect

  • Sometimes include congratulations or appreciation

  • Clearly state the purpose of writing

  • Use a smooth transition (bridge) from appreciation to the issue

Example

Dear Sir,
I wish to congratulate you on your appointment as Minister of Education. I also commend your efforts in improving the education sector. However, I would like to bring to your attention some challenges facing students in our school.

Body (Main Content)

The body contains the main points or problems. It must:
  • Be divided into clear paragraphs
  • Present one idea per paragraph
  • Explain each problem clearly
  • Show effects or consequences
  • Use formal and serious language

Structure:

  • Paragraph 1: First problem (e.g. poor infrastructure)
  • Paragraph 2: Second problem (e.g. lack of teachers)
  • Paragraph 3: Third problem (e.g. lack of learning materials)

Each paragraph should:

State the problem Explain it Show its effect on students

Conclusion (Closing Paragraph)

The conclusion must:
  • Summarize the main issues briefly
  • Restate the request or concern
  • End with a polite call to action
  • Show respect

Example idea:

I therefore kindly appeal to you to give urgent attention to these challenges in order to improve teaching and learning in our school. I look forward to your positive response.

SEMI-FORMAL LETTER

A semi-formal letter is between formal and informal. It is respectful but still personal.

Introduction (Opening Paragraph)

The introduction must:
  • Begin with respectful greeting
  • Show care or well-wishing
  • Include appreciation or respect (very important here)
  • State purpose clearly

Example idea:

Dear Uncle,
I hope you are in good health. I sincerely appreciate your continuous support and guidance in my education. I am writing to inform you about my academic progress.

Body (Main Content)

The body must:
  • Be respectful and clear
  • Avoid slang or informal expressions
  • Explain ideas properly
  • Keep each idea in separate paragraphs

Possible content:

  • Academic progress
  • Family updates
  • Requests or advice
  • Important information

Conclusion (Closing Paragraph)

The conclusion must:
  • Show respect and gratitude
  • End politely
  • Strengthen family or respectful bond

Example idea:

Thank you for your continuous support and care. I hope you will continue to guide me in my studies. Please accept my warm regards.

INFORMAL LETTER

An informal letter is friendly and personal. The tone is relaxed and conversational.

Introduction (Opening Paragraph)

The introduction must:
  • Begin with a friendly greeting
  • Show warmth and friendliness
  • Refer to shared experiences or past memories
  • State the purpose of writing clearly

Example idea:

Dear David,
I hope you are doing well. It has been a long time since we last met during your visit to my home, and I still remember the fun we had together. I am writing to invite you to my birthday party.

Body (Main Content)

The body must:
  • Be friendly and lively
  • Contain personal details or events
  • Use simple and compound sentences
  • Keep one idea per paragraph

Possible content:

  • Invitation details
  • Personal updates
  • Experiences or plans
  • Feelings and reactions

>Conclusion (Closing Paragraph)

The conclusion must:
  • End in a warm and friendly tone
  • Express hope of response or meeting
  • Maintain personal connection

Example idea:

I hope to see you at my birthday party. Take care and give my regards to your family.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS

  1. Formal Letters
    1. You are the school prefect in a school that does not have adequate toilet facilities for the pupils. Write a letter to your principal asking him/her to provide adequate toilet facilities highlighting the constraints you pupils face.
    2. People blame teachers for the failure of pupils in public examinations. Write a letter to the Minister of Education stating at least three ways in which pupils themselves contribute to their poor performance in public examinations.
    3. Most pupils in your school are regularly late because of transportation problems. Write a letter to your principal giving at least three reasons why your school needs a school bus.
    4. Write an application letter to the principal of your school for a place in SSS I (One) stating at least three reasons why you should be admitted.
    5. There has been an acute blackout in your community for weeks. Write a letter to the Manager of the Electricity Company complaining about how this is affecting you as a pupil.
    6. Pupils are now more interested in extra-curricular activities than academics. Write a letter to the Minister of Education, Science and Technology suggesting three ways of encouraging pupils to focus on their academic work.
    7. As the school's Head Boy/Head Girl, write a letter to your newly-appointed principal congratulating him/her and stating at least three urgent needs of the school.
    8. Write a letter to the newly elected chairperson of your local council congratulating him/her on his/her appointment and stating at least three urgent needs of the people of your community.
    9. Your school is badly in need of furniture. As Head Boy/Head Girl, write a letter of request to the Minister of Basic and Senior Secondary Education highlighting at least three ways in which pupils suffer due to this problem and suggest solutions.
    10. There has been a drop in passes in your school in public examinations. Write a letter to your principal suggesting three ways in which the situation can be improved.
    11. Examination centres are usually overcrowded by intruders during public examinations. Write a letter to the Minister of Education giving three reasons why intruders should not be allowed in examination centres.
    12. The Board of Governors of your school has awarded you a scholarship for your outstanding performance. Write a letter to the Chairperson thanking him/her and promising to live up to expectation.
    13. You wish to be appointed as Senior Prefect of your school. Write a letter to the Chairperson of the Prefect Appointment Committee giving at least three reasons why you are the most suitable candidate.
  2. Semi-formal Letters
    1. Your Language Arts teacher gave you homework for a week but you failed to do it. Write a letter of apology and give at least two reasons for your negligence.
    2. You have been suspended indefinitely for being rude to your class teacher. Write a letter of apology and reassure better behaviour in future.
    3. Your school has just won an Inter-School Debating Competition. Write a letter to your English teacher who is in hospital telling him/her about the victory, your role, and how the debate went.
    4. It is mandatory to attend your school's Prize Giving Ceremony. Write a letter of excuse to your class teacher giving at least three reasons why you cannot attend.
  3. Informal Letters
    1. Your mother insisted you attend Senior Secondary School in your town before leaving for Lagos. Write a letter giving reasons why you prefer another town.
    2. Write a letter to your friend describing your last School Sports Meeting and three interesting events.
    3. Your friend wants to attend your school for junior secondary. Write a reply giving reasons for or against the decision.
    4. Write to your brother abroad describing three positive changes the Local Council has made in your area.
    5. Write to a sponsor thanking him/her and stating your academic progress.
    6. Describe a class fight that led to suspensions and suggest two ways to prevent such occurrences.
    7. Your father wants you to change school location. Write giving reasons why you prefer staying with your mother.
    8. You did something your parents disapprove of. Write to your elder sister asking her to intervene on your behalf.
    9. Your friend is sick with malaria. Write a sympathy letter and explain prevention methods.
    10. Your uncle from America visited. Write to your friend describing three things he told you.
    11. Your parents are away, but your sister does not stay home. Write to them requesting their return.
    12. You are suspected of drug use. Write to your parents giving reasons you will never use harmful substances.
