READING AND COMPREHENSION

KEY IDEAS ABOUT READING COMPREHENSION

  • To understand a passage, a reader must be able to read words correctly and fluently.
  • Understanding also depends on vocabulary knowledge and meaning of words.
  • Good readers connect what they read with what they already know.
  • Reading is an active process where the reader thinks before, during, and after reading.
  • Knowing many words helps learners understand texts more easily.
  • Readers use clues in the passage to understand unfamiliar words.
  • Practice with different texts improves comprehension skills.
  • Readers should always try to understand the main message of what they read.

SKILLS USED IN READING COMPREHENSION

1. Using Prior Knowledge

Readers use what they already know to help them understand new information in a passage.

2. Predicting

Readers guess what the text will be about before or during reading based on hints and experience.

3. Identifying Main Ideas

Readers look for the most important idea in each paragraph and express it in their own words.

4. Asking Questions

Good readers ask questions before, during, and after reading to improve understanding.

5. Making Inferences

Readers use clues in the text and their own knowledge to understand meanings that are not clearly stated.

6. Visualising

Readers form mental pictures of what they are reading to help them understand and remember better.

READING TECHNIQUES

1. Skimming

Skimming means reading quickly to get the general idea of a passage.

How to skim:

  • Read the title
  • Read the introduction
  • Read the first sentence of each paragraph
  • Look at headings and subheadings
  • Look at pictures or diagrams
  • Read the conclusion or summary

2. Scanning

Scanning means reading quickly to find specific information such as names, dates, or answers.

How to scan:

  • Know what information you are looking for
  • Move your eyes quickly through the text
  • Focus only on key words and numbers
  • Read carefully only when you find the answer

STRUCTURE OF A COMPREHENSION PASSAGE

A comprehension passage is made up of paragraphs. Each paragraph contains sentences that explain one main idea.

  • Main sentence: Gives the central idea of the paragraph
  • Supporting sentences: Give details and explain the main idea

READING COMPREHENSION SKILLS: PRACTICAL GUIDE (HOW, WHEN AND WHERE TO USE THEM)

Reading comprehension is not just reading words. It is a skill of understanding, finding answers, and thinking about meaning. To succeed, you must use the right skill at the right time. Below is a practical guide showing how to use each skill step by step.

1. SKIMMING (GENERAL READING)

WHAT IT MEANS: Skimming means reading quickly to get the general idea of the passage.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • At the beginning, before answering questions
  • When you first see the passage in an exam

HOW TO USE IT (STEP BY STEP):

  1. Read the title first.
  2. Read the first paragraph quickly.
  3. Read the first sentence of each paragraph.
  4. Read the last paragraph quickly.
  5. Try to understand the main topic (not details).

WHERE TO FOCUS IN THE PASSAGE:

  • Title
  • Opening and closing sentences
  • Topic sentences of paragraphs

PRACTICAL USE: It helps you know what the passage is talking about before answering questions.

2. SCANNING (FINDING FACTS)

WHAT IT MEANS: Scanning means searching quickly for specific information.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • When answering questions like: Who? When? Where? How many?
  • When you need exact answers from the passage

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Look at the question first.
  2. Identify the keyword (name, date, place, number).
  3. Move your eyes quickly through the passage.
  4. Stop only when you see the keyword or related idea.
  5. Read the sentence carefully to confirm the answer.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Names of people
  • Dates and numbers
  • Places and events

PRACTICAL USE: It saves time in exams because you do not read everything again.

3. IDENTIFYING MAIN IDEA (KEY MESSAGE)

WHAT IT MEANS: Finding the most important idea in a paragraph or passage.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • After reading each paragraph
  • When answering “What is the passage about?”

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Read the paragraph carefully.
  2. Ask yourself: “What is the writer talking about here?”
  3. Look at the first sentence (often the main idea).
  4. Ignore extra details and examples.
  5. Summarise in one short sentence.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Topic sentence (usually first sentence)
  • Repeated ideas in the paragraph

PRACTICAL USE: It helps you understand the writer’s message clearly.

4. PREDICTING (GUESSING MEANING)

WHAT IT MEANS: Predicting means using clues to guess what will happen next or what the passage is about.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • Before reading the passage
  • While reading each paragraph

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Look at the title.
  2. Think about what the topic may be.
  3. Use your experience and knowledge.
  4. Read and check if your guess is correct.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Title
  • First paragraph clues

PRACTICAL USE: It prepares your mind before reading fully.


5. MAKING INFERENCES (READING BETWEEN THE LINES)

WHAT IT MEANS: Inferring means understanding meanings that are not directly written.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • When answers are not clearly stated in the passage
  • When questions ask “why do you think…” or “what can you say about…”

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Read the sentence carefully.
  2. Look for hidden clues in words or actions.
  3. Use your own knowledge to understand meaning.
  4. Combine clues and form a logical answer.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Character actions
  • Feelings and behaviour
  • Hints in the passage

PRACTICAL USE: It helps you answer difficult comprehension questions.


6. VISUALISING (CREATING MENTAL PICTURES)

WHAT IT MEANS: Visualising means forming a picture in your mind while reading.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • While reading descriptive passages

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Read slowly when describing people or places.
  2. Imagine the scene in your mind.
  3. Picture the actions happening.
  4. Use your imagination to understand better.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Descriptions of people
  • Events and actions
  • Scenes and places

PRACTICAL USE: It improves understanding and memory of the passage.


7. SUMMARISING (SHORT VERSION OF TEXT)

WHAT IT MEANS: Summarising means giving only the main points in your own words.

WHEN TO USE IT:

  • After reading the whole passage
  • When answering summary questions

HOW TO USE IT:

  1. Read the full passage carefully.
  2. Pick out main ideas from each paragraph.
  3. Remove unnecessary details.
  4. Rewrite using your own simple words.

WHERE TO FOCUS:

  • Main ideas of paragraphs
  • Key events only

PRACTICAL USE: It helps you revise and answer exam questions quickly.


READING COMPREHENSION PASSAGE: SIERRA LEONE

PASSAGE

Sierra Leone is a country in West Africa. It is known for its rich culture and history. The capital city is Freetown. Many people live in both rural and urban areas. The country has beautiful beaches and mountains. It is also rich in natural resources. English is the official language used in schools. The people of Sierra Leone are friendly and hardworking.


Freetown is the largest city in Sierra Leone. It was founded many years ago by freed slaves. The city is located near the Atlantic Ocean. Many important offices and businesses are found there. People in Freetown engage in trading and fishing. The city is busy during the day. There are schools, hospitals, and markets. Life in Freetown is active and colourful.


Agriculture is an important activity in Sierra Leone. Many people grow rice, cassava, and cocoa. Farmers work in villages across the country. The land is fertile in many regions. Fishing is also common in coastal areas. Some people work in mining and other industries. These activities help the economy grow. The government encourages people to farm more food.


Education is important in Sierra Leone. Children attend primary and secondary schools. Teachers help students learn different subjects. Schools are found in most communities. Students are encouraged to study hard. Education helps people get better jobs. The government supports education development. Many young people dream of becoming professionals.


QUESTIONS

  1. Where is Sierra Leone located?
  2. What is the capital city of Sierra Leone?
  3. Who founded Freetown?
  4. Give two natural features of Sierra Leone mentioned in the passage.
  5. What language is used in schools?
  6. Name two crops grown in Sierra Leone.
  7. Why is agriculture important?
  8. What do people in Freetown do for work?
  9. Why is education important?
  10. What do students dream of becoming?

READING COMPREHENSION SKILLS USING THE PASSAGE (SIERRA LEONE)

Below is how you apply each comprehension skill directly to the Sierra Leone passage. This shows you when, where, and how to use each skill in an exam.


PASSAGE REFERENCE: SIERRA LEONE COMPREHENSION

You have already read a passage about Sierra Leone with four paragraphs: - Paragraph 1: General information about the country - Paragraph 2: Freetown - Paragraph 3: Agriculture and economy - Paragraph 4: Education

1. SKIMMING (GENERAL IDEA)

WHEN TO USE IT: Before reading questions.

HOW TO USE IT IN THIS PASSAGE:

  • Read the title: “Sierra Leone”
  • Read quickly through each paragraph
  • Focus on the first sentence of each paragraph

WHAT YOU WILL UNDERSTAND:

  • The passage is about Sierra Leone (country description)
  • Topics include Freetown, farming, and education

PRACTICAL RESULT: You now know the passage is about life in Sierra Leone.


2. SCANNING (FINDING ANSWERS)

WHEN TO USE IT: When answering questions.

HOW TO USE IT IN THIS PASSAGE:

  • If asked “What is the capital?” scan Paragraph 1 and 2
  • If asked “What crops are grown?” scan Paragraph 3
  • If asked “Why is education important?” scan Paragraph 4

KEY WORDS TO LOOK FOR:

  • Freetown
  • rice, cassava, cocoa
  • education, schools

PRACTICAL RESULT: You find answers quickly without reading everything again.


3. MAIN IDEA (PARAGRAPH UNDERSTANDING)

WHEN TO USE IT: After reading each paragraph.

HOW TO USE IT:

  • Paragraph 1 → Sierra Leone overview
  • Paragraph 2 → Life in Freetown
  • Paragraph 3 → Agriculture and jobs
  • Paragraph 4 → Importance of education

PRACTICAL RESULT: You understand each paragraph’s message clearly.


4. PREDICTING (BEFORE READING DETAILS)

WHEN TO USE IT: Before reading the passage fully.

HOW TO USE IT HERE:

  • From the title “Sierra Leone”, predict it will talk about a country
  • Expect topics like cities, people, and schools

PRACTICAL RESULT: Your brain prepares for what you will read.


5. MAKING INFERENCES (READING BETWEEN LINES)

WHEN TO USE IT: When answers are not directly written.

EXAMPLE FROM PASSAGE:

  • “The people are friendly and hardworking” → You can infer they are kind and active in work
  • “Education helps people get better jobs” → You infer education improves life

PRACTICAL RESULT: You answer deeper questions that need thinking, not copying.


6. VISUALISING (CREATING MENTAL PICTURES)

WHEN TO USE IT: While reading descriptions.

IN THIS PASSAGE:

  • Imagine Freetown near the Atlantic Ocean
  • Picture farmers planting rice and cassava
  • Imagine students in classrooms studying

PRACTICAL RESULT: You understand and remember the passage better.


7. SUMMARISING (SHORT VERSION)

WHEN TO USE IT: After reading the whole passage.

HOW TO SUMMARISE THIS PASSAGE:

  • Sierra Leone is a West African country
  • Its capital is Freetown
  • People farm, fish, and mine
  • Education is important for development

RESULT: You can revise the whole passage in a few lines.


FINAL EXAM STRATEGY

  • Skim first (understand topic)
  • Read questions next
  • Scan for answers
  • Use inference for difficult questions
  • Summarise after reading
