Origin of Man

Traditional Beliefs (Myths and Legends)

Across cultures, origin stories have been handed down through generations to explain how humanity began.

  • The Limba (Sierra Leone)

    They believe that their god, Kanu Masala, descended from the sky, created the first man and first woman, and this couple gave birth to all humanity near the Wara Wara Mountains before spreading to other areas.


  • The Yoruba (Nigeria)

    In the beginning, only water existed. The supreme god, Olorun, sent his son Oduduwa with 16 chiefs carrying sand. A bird scattered the sand to form hills and valleys, and he ruled from Ile-Ife.

Religious Beliefs

Major world religions teach that a divine being created humans.

  • Christianity

    God created the universe in six days. The first man Adam was formed from dust, and the first woman Eve from Adam’s rib. They lived in the Garden of Eden.


  • Islam

    Allah created the first man Adam and first woman Hawwa. All humans descended from them.

Scientific Beliefs

The Theory of Evolution

Some scientists reject divine creation and propose that humans evolved over millions of years from primates. This idea was introduced by Charles Darwin in 1859 through Natural Selection.

Five key points of Darwin’s Theory

  • Overproduction – Organisms produce more offspring than can survive.
  • Variation – No two individuals are exactly alike.
  • Struggle for survival – Competition for food and space.
  • Survival of the fittest – Best adapted survive.
  • Origin of new species – New species evolve over time.

Cradle of Man

Africa: The Birthplace of Humanity

Africa is regarded as the cradle of humankind because:

  • Oldest fossils have been found in Africa.
  • Africa had a warm and wet climate suitable for early humans.

Key Terms

  • Fossils – preserved remains of ancient life.
  • Archaeologist – studies ancient human remains.
  • Paleoanthropologist – studies human evolution fossils.

The Five Stages of Human Development

  1. Proconsul (Australopithecus)

    Lived about 4.2 million years ago in East Africa. They were smaller than modern humans and ate fruits, nuts, roots, and small animals.

  2. Homo habilis

    Lived between 3 million and 1.8 million years ago. They had larger brains and were the first to use tools and their thumbs for gripping. A famous fossil is Lucy found in Ethiopia.

  3. Homo erectus

    Lived between 1.5 million and 100,000 years ago. They walked , used fire, made better tools, and developed simple language. They were the first to migrate out of Africa.

  4. Homo sapiens

    Lived between 100,000 and 40,000 years ago. They had larger brains, straight legs, and heavy brows. Fossils have been found in Africa and .

  5. Homo sapiens sapiens

    This is modern humans (us). We have flat faces, high foreheads, and smaller brow ridges.

Comparing Humans and Animals

Similarities

  • Primates

    Humans share similar limbs and internal organs with monkeys and apes.

  • Vertebrates

    Humans have a backbone just like dogs and chimpanzees.

  • Mammals

    Humans give live birth and feed their young with breast milk.

Differences

  • We walk upright.
  • We have advanced brains.
  • We use tools.
  • We use complex language.

The Human Brain

  • Cerebrum

    Controls thinking, seeing, hearing, movement, and speaking.

  • Cerebellum

    Controls body balance and helps prevent falling.

  • Medulla

    Controls automatic body functions like the heart and lungs.

The Human Race

All humans belong to one human race and originated from Africa.

  • Negroid

    People with dark skin adapted to strong sunlight in Africa.

  • Caucasoid

    People with light skin adapted to cooler regions with less sunlight.

  • Mongoloid

    People with yellowish skin and straight black hair; some adapted to cold climates.

World Population Trend

Europe experienced steady population growth due to the agricultural revolution and industrial revolution, which improved food production and industry.

Africa has high birth rates and death rates, but many areas are now experiencing rapid urban population growth.

Factors Affecting Population

  • Climate – affects food supply and living conditions.
  • Disease – causes deaths and reduces population growth.
  • War – leads to loss of lives and displacement.
  • Food production – increases or decreases survival rate.
  • Marriage patterns – early marriage increases population growth.

Problems of Population Growth

  • Unemployment – too many people competing for few jobs.
  • Low standard of living – shortage of resources.
  • Crime – increases due to poverty and joblessness.
  • Prostitution – caused by economic hardship.

Population Types

  • Underpopulation – fewer people than available resources.
  • Optimum population – population matches available resources.
  • Overpopulation – more people than available resources.

Causes of Overpopulation

  • High birth rate – more births increase population.
  • Immigration – people moving into a country increase population.
  • Improved healthcare – reduces death rate.
  • Early marriage – leads to more children.
  • Low death rate – more people survive longer.

Population Census

A census is the counting of all people in a country at a specific time.

Importance

  • Planning – helps government plan services.
  • Resource distribution – ensures fair sharing of resources.
  • Taxes – helps in revenue planning.
  • Policy making – supports decision making.

Problems

  • Political interference – affects accuracy.
  • High cost – expensive to conduct.
  • Poor communication – difficult access to some areas.
  • Illiteracy – people may not understand questions.

Types of Census

  • De facto: counting people physically present
  • De jure: counting people based on residence

Data Collection Methods

  • Census – the official counting of all people in a country at a particular time. It provides information about population size, age, sex, occupation and location.
  • Civil Registration – the continuous recording of important life events such as births, deaths, marriages and divorces.
  • Health Records – records collected by hospitals and health centres about births, deaths, diseases and other health information.
  • Sample Surveys – a method of collecting information from a selected group of people who represent the larger population. It is faster and less expensive than a census.

Institutions for Population Data

  • Statistics Sierra Leone – the government agency responsible for conducting censuses, surveys and producing official population statistics.
  • National Electoral Commission – collects voter registration data and provides information about the voting-age population.
  • Immigration Department – records information about people entering and leaving the country and helps track migration patterns.
  • Births and Deaths Registry – registers births and deaths and provides important population statistics.
  • National Registration Office – issues national identity cards and maintains records of citizens and residents.

Communication

Communication is the transmission of information and the exchange of thoughts, ideas and feelings between people.

Forms of Communication

  • Verbal Communication – communication through spoken words to express thoughts, ideas and feelings.
  • Non-Verbal Communication – communication through written materials, sounds, signs, gestures, pictures and symbols.

Means of Verbal Communication

  • Face-to-face speaking – direct conversation between people.
  • Telephone – communication over a distance using phones.
  • Radio and Television – communication through broadcasts.

Traditional Means of Communication

  • Drums – used to gather people and send messages.
  • Fire and Smoke – used to send simple messages.
  • Town Crier – announces important information to the community.
  • Symbols – signs used to communicate messages.
  • Gestures – body movements used for communication.
  • Signals – signs used to convey information.
  • Messengers – people sent to deliver messages.

Telecommunication

Telecommunication means communication at a distance.

  • Telegraph – sends messages over long distances.
  • Telephone – allows people to talk over long distances.
  • Radar – detects and communicates information about objects.
  • Satellites – transmit information across large distances.

Mass Media

Mass Media refers to the various ways information is made available to a large number of people.

Print Media
  • Books – printed sources of information.
  • Journals – publications containing articles and information.
  • Newspapers – provide current news and information.
  • Magazines – periodicals containing articles and pictures.
  • Posters – printed notices for public information.
  • Pictures – visual means of communication.
Electronic Media
  • Radio – broadcasts information through sound.
  • Television – broadcasts information through sound and pictures.
  • Computer – electronic device used for communication and information.
  • Cinema – communication through motion pictures.

Special Means of Communication

  • Signs and Pictures – used by the deaf and dumb.
  • Braille – used by the blind for reading and writing.

Postal Services

Postal Services are ways in which people deliver written messages through the post office.

  • Letters and Parcels – received and dispatched by the post office.
  • Telegrams – sent through postal services.
  • Stamps and Airmails – sold to the public.

Transportation

Transportation is the movement of people, goods and services from one place to another.

Forms of Transportation

  • Land Transport – movement on land using roads and railways.
  • Water Transport – movement through rivers, lakes and seas.
  • Air Transport – movement through the air.

Land Transportation

  • Human Power – movement by walking and cycling.
  • Beasts of Burden – animals such as camels, horses, oxen and elephants used for transport.
  • Motor Vehicles – engine-powered vehicles such as cars, buses and trucks.
  • Trains – vehicles running on railway tracks.
  • Wheeled Vehicles – vehicles that move on wheels such as bicycles and carts.

Advantages of Land Transportation

  • Flexible – can reach towns and villages by road.
  • Direct Movement – carries goods directly from producers to markets.
  • Less Expensive – cheaper than air transportation.
  • Promotes Trade – encourages buying and selling of goods.

Disadvantages of Land Transportation

  • Expensive Construction – roads are costly and difficult to build.
  • High Maintenance Cost – roads and bridges require frequent repairs.
  • Accidents – road accidents occur frequently.
  • Slow – not as fast as air transportation.

Water Transportation

Water Transportation is the movement of people and goods through rivers, lakes and seas.

  • Boats – transport people and goods on water.
  • Ferries – carry passengers, vehicles and goods across water bodies.

Advantages of Water Transportation

  • Carries Bulky Goods – transports large and heavy goods.
  • Carries Many Passengers – can transport many people at once.
  • Less Expensive – relatively cheap compared to air transport.

Disadvantages of Water Transportation

  • Slowest Means – takes a long time to reach destinations.
  • Irregular Movement – services may not operate regularly.
  • Frequent Delays – journeys are often delayed.

Air Transportation

Air Transportation is the fastest means of transport using airplanes and helicopters.


Airplanes – transport passengers and goods through the air.

Advantages of Air Transportation

  • Fastest – quickest means of transportation.
  • Perishable Goods – suitable for goods that spoil quickly.
  • Employment – provides jobs for people.
  • Trade – promotes local and international trade.

Disadvantages of Air Transportation

  • Very Expensive – costs more than other forms of transport.
  • Bulky Goods Costly – expensive to transport heavy goods.
  • Airspace Restrictions – affected by national airspace regulations.
  • Bad Weather – weather conditions can disrupt flights.
  • Fatal Accidents – air crashes are often deadly.
