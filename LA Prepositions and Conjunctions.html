PREPOSITIONS AND CONJUNCTIONS

Prepositions and conjunctions are important parts of grammar. They help us to show relationships between words and join ideas in sentences.


PREPOSITIONS

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. It often shows place, time, or direction.


1. SIMPLE PREPOSITIONS

Meaning: These are single-word prepositions.

Examples: in, on, at, by, to, from, under, over, with, without, between, through

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • The book is on the table.
  • The cat is under the chair.
  • She sat in the room quietly.
  • He walked through the gate.
  • The teacher is at the school.

2. COMPOUND PREPOSITIONS

Meaning: These are prepositions made up of more than one word.

Examples: in front of, because of, in between, on behalf of, out of

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • The shop is in front of the school.
  • He failed because of illness.
  • The bank is in between the market and the post office.
  • She spoke on behalf of the class.
  • The dog ran out of the house.

3. PREPOSITIONS OF MOVEMENT

Meaning: They show movement from one place to another.

Examples: to, into, through, across

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • She went to school early.
  • The boy jumped into the river.
  • He walked across the road carefully.
  • The train passed through the tunnel.
  • They moved to a new city.

4. PREPOSITIONS OF PLACE

Meaning: They show where something is located.

Examples: in, on, at, under, behind, beside, above, below

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • The bag is on the chair.
  • The dog is under the table.
  • He is in the room.
  • The school is behind the church.
  • The lamp is above the table.

5. PREPOSITIONS OF TIME

Meaning: They show when something happens.

Examples: at, on, in

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • She comes to school at 7:00 am.
  • The meeting is on Monday.
  • He was born in 2010.
  • We go to church on Sunday.
  • I will travel in December.


CONJUNCTIONS

A conjunction is a word that joins words, phrases, or sentences together.


1. COORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS

Meaning: They join two equal ideas.

Examples: and, but, or, so, yet, nor

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • I like rice and beans.
  • She is poor but happy.
  • Study hard or you will fail.
  • He was tired yet he continued working.
  • It was raining so we stayed inside.

2. SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS

Meaning: They join a main clause and a dependent clause.

Examples: because, if, although, when, before, till, that

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • I stayed at home because I was sick.
  • If it rains, we will stay inside.
  • Although he was tired, he worked hard.
  • I will wait till you come back.
  • She said that she was ready.

3. CORRELATIVE CONJUNCTIONS

Meaning: These are paired conjunctions that work together.

Examples: either...or, neither...nor, both...and, whether...or

EXAMPLES IN SENTENCES:

  • Either John or Musa will come.
  • It is neither good nor bad.
  • Both Mary and Aminata are present.
  • I do not know whether he will come or not.
  • Either study hard or fail the exam.
